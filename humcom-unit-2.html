HUMCOM — UNIT 2

The Interaction Design Process

Take Quiz 2.1 →

Unit 2 Overview — What to Expect

Apply the five non-linear stages of Design Thinking, conduct UX evaluations using Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics, and build fast low-fidelity prototypes.

Learning Objectives

  • Execute the five stages of Design Thinking (Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test) and craft Point-of-View (POV) statements.
  • Evaluate interfaces across Usability, Desirability, and Accessibility using Nielsen's 10 Usability Heuristics.
  • Select appropriate low-fidelity prototyping methods (Sketches, Paper Prototypes, Storyboards, Wireframes, Wizard of Oz).

Lesson 2.1: Design Thinking

Jump to Quiz 2.1 ↓
Key Reminder: Premature Judgment Warning: During Ideate sessions, defer criticism to encourage wide idea diversity before filtering feasibility.

The Five Non-Linear Stages of Design Thinking

  1. Empathize: Observe and interview real users to uncover explicit and implicit needs.
  2. Define: Synthesize findings into a Point-of-View (POV) statement ([User] needs a way to [need] because [insight]) and How Might We (HMW) questions.
  3. Ideate: Brainstorm diverse solutions (Crazy 8s, SCAMPER) while deferring judgment.
  4. Prototype: Build rough, low-cost, disposable representations to test assumptions.
  5. Test: Observe real users interacting with prototypes; loop back to Define or Ideate as needed.

Lesson 2.2: UX Evaluation & Nielsen's 10 Heuristics

Jump to Quiz 2.2 ↓
Key Reminder: Cryptic Error Warning: Displaying 'Error 42' without plain-language diagnosis and recovery steps violates Heuristic #9.

Three Pillars of UX & Nielsen's 10 Heuristics

UX Evaluation balances Usability ("Can they use it?"), Desirability ("Do they like it?"), and Accessibility ("Can everyone use it?"). In a Heuristic Evaluation, 3 to 5 independent evaluators inspect an interface against Jakob Nielsen's 10 Heuristics: (1) Visibility of system status, (2) Match between system & real world, (3) User control & freedom (Undo), (4) Consistency & standards, (5) Error prevention, (6) Recognition rather than recall, (7) Flexibility & efficiency, (8) Aesthetic & minimalist design, (9) Help users recognize, diagnose & recover from errors, and (10) Help & documentation.

Lesson 2.3: Low-Fidelity Prototyping

Jump to Quiz 2.3 ↓
Key Reminder: Ethical Debriefing: When conducting Wizard of Oz tests where a human secretly simulates AI/automation, always debrief participants ethically afterward.

Five Low-Fidelity Prototyping Techniques

MethodCore Purpose
SketchesFastest freehand exploration of multiple concepts at virtually zero cost.
Paper PrototypesHand-drawn screens swapped by a facilitator to test navigation flow and labels early.
StoryboardsComic-strip narrative showing physical environment, context, and user emotion over time.
WireframesGrayscale structural skeletons showing hierarchy and layout without branding polish.
Wizard of OzInterface appears automated/AI-driven while a human researcher operates responses behind the scenes.
