HUMCOM — UNIT 6

Usability & UX Goals, Presentation, and Ethics

Take Quiz 6.1 →

Unit 6 Overview — What to Expect

Synthesize all six pragmatic usability goals, defend design decisions using the HCI Defense Script, conduct severity-rated peer reviews, and uphold ethical gatekeeper standards.

Learning Objectives

  • Audit systems across all six usability goals (Effective, Efficient, Safe, Utility, Learnable, Memorable) and justify trade-offs.
  • Deliver a structured 10-minute HCI presentation using the HCI Defense Script and Nielsen's 0–4 severity scale.
  • Eliminate dark patterns (roach motels, confirm-shaming, pre-ticked consent) under the ACM Code of Ethics.

Lesson 6.1: Usability Goals & Project Defense Trade-Offs

The Six Pragmatic Usability Goals (Preece, Rogers & Sharp)

1. Effective (accuracy & task completion %), 2. Efficient (minimal time/clicks), 3. Safe (error prevention & undo windows), 4. Utility (right features for real needs), 5. Learnable (fast first-time onboarding), and 6. Memorable (easy recall after months away).

Lesson 6.2: UX Goals, Presentation & Ethics in HCI

Jump to Quiz 6.2 ↓
Key Reminder: Dark Pattern Prohibition: Never use 'roach motel' cancellation traps, confirm-shaming, or pre-ticked data consent boxes.

The HCI Defense Script & Nielsen Severity Scale (0–4)

HCI Defense Script: "I chose [design decision] because according to [HCI principle], users tend to [human behavior]. This ensures we reduce [cognitive load/friction] and improve [usability/conversion]."

Nielsen Severity Ratings: 0 = Not a problem, 1 = Cosmetic, 2 = Minor, 3 = Major (high priority), 4 = Usability catastrophe (imperative to fix before launch).

Ethics in HCI: Designers as Gatekeepers

Under the ACM Code of Ethics, designers reject dark patterns, protect data privacy with explicit opt-in consent, include stopping cues for mental health, and attribute user mistakes to Gulf of Execution design gaps rather than "user stupidity."
